# Recon — FRAME & ACTION subsystem

Scope: receiver body behind the barrel, hammer / rear spur, trigger, trigger guard,
breech / rear cap, and the cylindrical port on top toward the rear.

Nothing here is modelled. Every number below is a pixel measurement with its method,
or is marked DECLARED.

---

## 0. Coordinate conventions used throughout

All pixel coordinates are **sheet coordinates** of `../jinx-i2t/ref/pose_gun_5view.jpg`
(3000x1462). The four crops are byte-identical sub-rectangles of that sheet
(verified by `cv2.matchTemplate`, TM_CCOEFF_NORMED = 1.0000 for all four):

| crop | sheet origin | size |
|---|---|---|
| `ref/gun_pose0.png` | (42, 150) | 225 x 160 |
| `ref/gun_pose1.png` | (642, 192) | 215 x 150 |
| `ref/gun_pose2.png` | (1650, 203) | 160 x 129 |
| `ref/gun_pose3.png` | (2421, 214) | 236 x 118 |

So `crop_xy = sheet_xy - origin`, and a pixel in a crop is a pixel in the sheet.
Working in the sheet gives extra *context* (not extra detail) — important for pose3,
whose receiver and port are **cut off by the crop's left edge** (they live at
crop-local x = -13 .. +24).

**Axial parameter t.** Measured along the barrel axis in the pose3 side view:

- `t = 0` at the muzzle face (frontmost gun pixel), sheet x = 2603
- `t = 1` at the receiver rear face (rearmost receiver pixel), sheet x = 2415
- `L = 188 px`, `t(x) = (2603 - x) / 188`

Axial *ratios* measured inside one view are invariant to axial foreshortening, which is
why everything below is quoted as t first and pixels second.

**Pose usability for this subsystem** (measured, not asserted):

| pose | projected gun length (muzzle to rear) | foreshortening vs pose3 | frame usable? |
|---|---|---|---|
| pose0 | 62 -> 205 = 143 px | 0.76 | rear 3/4; port + spur visible, everything below the receiver is inside the fist |
| pose1 | 683 -> 836 = 153 px | 0.81 | spur broadside; frame is dark-on-dark; trigger area behind the hand |
| pose2 | near muzzle-on | — | frame is behind the barrel, unusable |
| pose3 | 2415 -> 2603 = 188 px | 1.00 (reference) | **best**: cleanest silhouette step, port and receiver both readable |

Barrel axis in pose3: slope **+0.055 px/px** (3.1 deg nose-down toward the muzzle at
image-right), fitted from the tube's upper and lower silhouette lines over x 2490-2570
(`analysis/work_fa/axis3.py`). Axis height `y_axis(2431) = 265 +/- 3`.

Main tube outside diameter in pose3: **41 +/- 4 px** (column dumps at x = 2500 / 2510 /
2520; tube top y 252, bottom y 295 at x = 2520 -> 44 px; 37 px at x = 2500 where the
brass rear band sits). The **top rail is a separate 7 px-thick bar sitting on the tube**
(at x 2520: warm brass y 245-251, dark seam 252-256, tube grey-teal from 257) — any
silhouette-only "tube diameter" that tops out at y 246 is measuring rail + tube.

The lattice collar's rear face is the last barrel feature. Its rear edge is the
silhouette step at **x = 2445 +/- 1.5** — confirmed visually in
`analysis/work_fa/p3_top.png`, where the bright yellow rail bar and the first dark
lozenge cutout both begin at x 2446, with 7 px of clean background at x 2438-2445

> **Barrel / frame junction: `t = 0.840 +/- 0.03` (sheet x = 2445 +/- 1.5, pose3).**
> **Frame + action axial length: 30 px = `0.160 +/- 0.03` of L.**

Caveat, stated plainly: this is *"the rearmost barrel feature ends here"*. The receiver
could continue forward **underneath** the collar; the collar wraps the tube and hides
whatever is behind it. I cannot see the receiver's forward face in any pose.

Cross-check attempts in pose1 fail to converge, and this is worth recording because it
looks like a disagreement and is not: pose1's collar is ~34 deg out of plane, so its near
and far rims separate in projection and the "step" can be read at x 797 (t=0.745),
x 823 (t=0.915) or x 820 (frame-colour boundary, t=0.895) depending on which edge you
pick. Pose1 cannot settle this boundary; pose3 can. Tolerance widened to +/-0.03 to cover it.

so its axial fractions carry perspective error; quoted as **0.90 +/- 0.04**.

projecting up and rearward. Confirmed in three poses; measurable in one.

**pose1 — broadside, the only view that gives its shape.** PCA on the brass-classified
pixels in sheet box x 824-860, y 236-263 (HSV: S>50, V>85, 5<=H<=32; 238 px):

- centroid (843.4, 248.7)
- principal axis **34.3 deg above the image horizontal**, pointing up and rearward
- **length 32.7 px, width 11.8 px -> aspect 2.77**
- bounding box x 824-856, y 238-259
- pose1's barrel axis is -6.9 deg (rises toward the rear), so the spur is
  **27.4 deg above the barrel axis** *in pose1's projection*

**pose0** — brass at x 191-207, y 209-222 (16 x 13 px), immediately rearward of the port,
running down-and-rearward before the fist swallows it at x ~ 207.

**pose3** — a 2 px-thick tapering sliver, x 2408 -> 2422 at y 256-259, projecting
rearward from the receiver's top-rear corner, ~4 deg below the horizontal
(`analysis/work_fa/p3_streak.png`).

**Its attitude is NOT determined.** Pose1 projects it at +27 deg to the barrel axis;
pose3 projects it at -7 deg. A 34 deg disagreement between two side-ish views means the
spur has a large out-of-plane component, or that pose1 and pose3 show opposite sides of
the gun and the element is asymmetric. I can measure the spur's broadside outline; I
cannot measure the plane it lies in.

**Note on the pose1 measurement.** In a whole-sheet background-subtracted segmentation the
spur comes back as its **own connected component** (bbox x 826-857, y 238-260, area 361
px) — it joins the receiver only through pixels within ~20 grey levels of the background.
Its neck is at or below the resolution of this reference.

## 5. Trigger and trigger guard — NOT RESOLVED

This is the correct answer, not a failure to look. The trigger and its guard are behind
the shooting hand in every pose on the sheet:

- **pose0** — the fist (black glove) starts at y = 240; the receiver's underside is at
  y ~ 235. There are **zero pixels** of trigger or guard.
- **pose1** — the shooting hand occupies x > 835; the extended index finger lies across
  the frame's underside at y 285-300. The only candidate is a 2-3 px low-contrast linear
  feature from (794, 300) to (810, 316) — `t = 0.73 ... 0.83` — sloping down and rearward
  at ~45 deg, with a lit upper edge and a shadowed underside
  (`analysis/work_fa/p1_guard_amp.png`, background-subtracted and 4x amplified). It is
  equally consistent with a guard bow, the grip's front strap, or a cast shadow. I will
  not turn 2 px into a constraint.
- **pose3** — the fingers begin at y ~ 296, immediately under the receiver.
- **pose2** — the frame is behind the barrel in a near-muzzle-on view.
- **figure 5** on the sheet (sheet x 2612-2929) does hold the gun, but muzzle-on beside
  her head with the frame behind her hair (`analysis/work_fa/fig5_gun2.png`). Useless.

**One thing about the guard *can* be constrained, negatively.** In pose3 the region below

(`analysis/work_fa/p3_guard.png`). So **nothing belonging to the gun hangs below the
barrel forward of `t = 0.71`**. That is falsifiable and cheap to check. (See FA-17.)

## 7. The scale chain is wrong by a factor of about 1.7

The brief asked for this to be attacked. It does not survive.

**The premise misreads its own source.** In `../jinx-i2t/baseline/spec_baseline.json`:

```
hand-l        { width 0.044, height 0.092, depth 0.028 }
index-l-1..3  { width 0.016, height 0.022, depth 0.016 }   (same for middle/ring/little)
```

`hand-l` is the **palm block**. The four fingers are separate components, 0.016 m wide
each. Four fingers side by side in that same file = **4 x 0.016 = 0.064 m**, not 0.044 m.
The chain's "44 mm across the four fingers" is the palm's width used as if it were the
finger span. Reading the same source consistently multiplies every derived dimension by
**64/44 = 1.45**.

**The pixel span is also soft.** I re-measured the four-finger stack in the best-resolved
hand on the sheet — pose3's, where all four fingers are separated and each carries a
painted nail (`analysis/work_fa/p3_hand.png`): top of index y = 296 to bottom of little
finger y = 341 -> **45 px** (sheet x 2410-2445). Pose1 gives 59 px, but pose1's index is
extended off the grip and displaced. The chain's 52.5 px sits between the two; call the
span 45-59 px, i.e. +/-15%.

**An independent anchor agrees with the corrected chain, not the original.** Whole-sheet
background-subtracted connected components give figure silhouette heights of
**1207 / 1220 / 1192 px** (figures 1, 2, 5). The baseline spec's own segment sum —
foot 0.07 + shin 0.441 + thigh 0.397 + pelvis 0.14 + abdomen 0.19 + chest 0.16 +
neck 0.076 + head 0.169 — is **1.643 m**; plus roughly 0.10 m of hair above the crown
gives a 1.74 m silhouette.

| anchor | mm/px |
|---|---|
| current chain (0.044 m / 52.5 px) | **0.838** |
| corrected finger span (0.064 m / 45 px, pose3) | **1.42** |
| corrected finger span (0.064 m / 52.5 px) | 1.22 |
| figure silhouette (1.74 m / 1220 px) | **1.43** |

The two corrected anchors agree to 1%. The original agrees with nothing: at 0.838 mm/px
the figure would be 1220 x 0.838 = **1.02 m tall**, against the same file's own 1.643 m
of stacked segments.

**Third check, from the same spec file.** Its `zapper` block is
`{ width 0.07, height 0.30, depth 0.09 }` — a 0.30 m long axis. My measured
muzzle-to-breech length is 188 px; at 1.42 mm/px that is **267 mm**, becoming ~0.30 m once
grip and spur overhang are included. At 0.838 mm/px it would be 158 mm, which the spec's
own 0.30 m contradicts. (That same file's `zapper-barrel width 0.038` is consistent with
the *old* scale, so the character blockout inherited the same error.)

**Consequence:** every absolute millimetre figure derived from 0.838 mm/px is low by
~1.7x. Main tube OD becomes ~58 mm rather than 30 mm; the muzzle collar ~65 mm; the
frame's axial length ~43 mm. This is why every constraint in §8 is stated as a ratio
first, with the millimetre value quoted separately and flagged as inheriting the chain.

## 8. Proposed constraints

Checking conventions, assumed once for all of them:

- `axis` = barrel axis unit vector, muzzle-forward.
- `a(p) = (p - P_muzzle_face) . (-axis)` — axial coordinate, 0 at the muzzle face,
  increasing rearward.
- `L = a(rearmost vertex of the receiver group)`; `t(p) = a(p) / L`.
- `D_tube` = the main tube mesh's largest extent perpendicular to `axis`.
- `up` = the gun's local up (perpendicular to `axis`, opposite the grip's drop).
- All checks read **per-mesh world-space AABBs** from the built scene, except FA-15 and
  FA-16 which need a silhouette or top-down render.

---

**FA-1 `frame.junction.t`** — `ratio` — value **0.840**, tol **+/-0.03**
The rearmost barrel-group feature (lattice collar) ends at t = 0.840; everything at
larger t is frame/action.
*Check:* take `max a(v)` over all vertices of the barrel group, divide by L; assert
0.810 <= result <= 0.870.
*Evidence:* pose3, silhouette step at sheet x = 2445 +/- 1.5; corroborated by the rail bar
and the first lozenge both starting at x 2446 (`analysis/work_fa/p3_top.png`).
*Confidence:* 0.7

**FA-2 `frame.axial.fraction`** — `ratio` — **0.160**, tol **+/-0.03**
The frame + action group spans 16% of the muzzle-to-breech axial length.
*Check:* `(L - a(collar rear face)) / L`.
*Evidence:* pose3, x 2445 -> 2415 = 30 px, of L = 188 px.
*Confidence:* 0.7

**FA-3 `frame.port.t`** — `ratio` — **0.90**, tol **+/-0.04**
The port's axis crosses the barrel axis at t = 0.90.
*Check:* project the port mesh's AABB centre onto `axis`, take t.
*Evidence:* pose3 centre x 2431 -> 0.915; pose0 centre x 185 -> 0.87.
*Confidence:* 0.65

**FA-4 `frame.port.od_over_tube_od`** — `ratio` — **0.34**, tol **+/-0.06**
*Check:* port AABB extent perpendicular to both `axis` and `up`, divided by `D_tube`.
*Evidence:* port OD 15 px (pose3, x 2424-2438) and 12 px (pose0, x 179-191);
D_tube 41 +/- 4 px (pose3 column dumps at x 2500/2510/2520).
*Confidence:* 0.6

**FA-5 `frame.port.h_over_od`** — `ratio` — **1.3**, tol **+/-0.4**
Port height above the receiver shoulder divided by its own OD.
*Check:* `(port AABB max along up - receiver AABB max along up) / port_OD`.
*Evidence:* pose3 15/15 = 1.0; pose0 18/12 = 1.5, or 21/12 = 1.7 after correcting for the
29 deg camera elevation implied by the rim ellipse (minor/major = 6/12).
*Confidence:* 0.5

**FA-6 `frame.port.gap_to_collar`** — `relation` + `ratio` — the port does **not** touch
the lattice collar; axial gap = **0.037 L**, tol **+/-0.02 L**
*Check:* AABB overlap test along `axis` between the port and the collar must be disjoint,
and `t(port front face) - t(collar rear face)` in [0.017, 0.057].
*Evidence:* pose3, 7 px of clean background at sheet x 2438-2445, y 236-248.
*Confidence:* 0.7

**FA-7 `frame.port.axis_perpendicular`** — `angle` — the port's axis is within **15 deg**
of `up`, with lateral component `|port_axis . (axis x up)| < 0.26`
*Check:* dot products of the port mesh's principal axis against `up` and the lateral vector.
*Evidence:* pose0, port silhouette sides parallel and vertical to +/-1 px over y 208-217
(x 180 and x 190 constant); pose3, x 2425 and x 2438 constant to +/-1 px over y 238-247.
The residual 13 deg forward lean read in pose3 falls inside this tolerance.
*Confidence:* 0.6

**FA-8 `frame.protrusions_above.count`** — `count` — **2**
Exactly two things stand above the receiver's top surface in the frame's axial span
(t > 0.84): the port and the rear spur. No third.
*Check:* count meshes whose AABB min along `up` exceeds `receiver AABB max along up`
minus 0.10 * D_tube, restricted to `t(AABB centre) > 0.84`. Must equal 2.
*Evidence:* pose0 sheet x 155-207, pose1 x 805-857, pose3 x 2405-2445 — one brass cylinder
and one brass slab in each, nothing else.
*Confidence:* 0.75

**FA-9 `frame.port_forward_of_spur`** — `relation`
The port's axial centre is **forward of** the rear spur's.
*Check:* `t(port AABB centre) < t(spur AABB centre)`.
*Evidence:* pose0 port x 179-191, spur x 191-207 (rear = +x); pose3 port x 2424-2438, spur
x 2408-2422 (rear = -x). Both poses agree, and they view opposite ends of the gun, so this
is not an artefact of one camera.
*Confidence:* 0.7

**FA-10 `frame.spur.aspect`** — `ratio` — **2.8**, tol **+/-0.7**
Rear spur broadside length divided by broadside width.
*Check:* spur mesh AABB longest extent / second-longest extent.
*Evidence:* pose1 PCA over 238 brass pixels — 32.7 px along the principal axis, 11.8 px
across.
*Confidence:* 0.55

**FA-11 `frame.spur.len_over_tube_od`** — `ratio` — **0.73**, tol **+/-0.18**
*Check:* spur AABB longest extent / `D_tube`.
*Evidence:* pose1 spur length 32.7 px against pose1's tube silhouette height 45 px
(x 723-744) — same image, same scale, both perpendicular-ish measures.
*Confidence:* 0.5

**FA-12 `frame.top_above_axis_over_tube_od`** — `ratio` — **0.29**, tol **+/-0.09**
Height of the receiver's top surface above the barrel axis, over `D_tube`. Chosen because
it avoids the occluded underside entirely.
*Check:* `(receiver AABB max along up) / D_tube`, with the axis at up = 0.
*Evidence:* pose3, receiver top y 253 at x 2431, `y_axis(2431) = 265 +/- 3`, D_tube 41 +/- 4.
*Confidence:* 0.6

**FA-13 `frame.top_below_barrel_top`** — `relation` + `ratio` **0.12**, tol **+/-0.08**
The receiver's top surface is **below** the main tube's top line; the drop, over `D_tube`,
is 0.12.
*Check:* `receiver AABB max along up < tube AABB max along up`, and the difference over
`D_tube` in [0.04, 0.20].
*Evidence:* pose3 — tube top y 252 at x 2520, slope 0.055 -> y 247.6 at x 2431; receiver
top y 253 there; drop 5 +/- 3 px.
*Confidence:* 0.5

**FA-14 `frame.height_over_tube_od`** — `ratio` — **>= 0.95** (lower bound, not a target)
*Check:* `(receiver AABB extent along up) / D_tube >= 0.95`.
*Evidence:* pose3 receiver visible height 41 +/- 5 px (y 253 -> 294) against D_tube 41 +/- 4.
The lower edge is where the hand starts, so the true frame can only be taller. Stated as an
inequality for that reason.
*Confidence:* 0.45

**FA-15 `frame.rear_face.angle`** — `angle` — the receiver's rear face is within **15 deg**
of perpendicular to `axis`, over its lower 60%
*Check:* render an orthographic side silhouette; fit a line to the rearmost boundary of the
receiver group between 20% and 80% of its height; compare with the axis normal.
*Evidence:* pose3 rear silhouette holds x 2414-2416 from y 266 to y 290 — 2 px of drift
over 25 px = 4.6 deg, before allowing for pose3's own obliquity.
*Confidence:* 0.5

**FA-16 `frame.port.hollow`** — `relation` + `ratio` inner/outer **0.45**, tol **+/-0.15**
The port's top face is recessed: an annular rim around a visible bore.
*Check:* orthographic top-down render of the port alone — the silhouette must contain a
hole, and `bore_diameter / port_OD` in [0.30, 0.60]. Equivalently: a ray cast down the
port's axis enters more than one surface.
*Evidence:* pose0 raw luminance, rows 203-204 — bright arc at x 182 (142) and x 188-189
(131, 133), darker interior x 183-187 (69-89). **This is 5 interior pixels against a 2 px
rim, in one pose.** A domed solid top with a specular ring would look the same.
*Confidence:* 0.4

**FA-17 `action.nothing_below_barrel_forward_of_071`** — `relation`
No part of the gun extends below the tube's lower surface at t < 0.71. This is the only
hard statement I can make about the trigger guard's forward reach.
*Check:* for every mesh, if `t(AABB centre) < 0.71` then `AABB min along up` must be above
`tube AABB min along up` minus 0.05 * D_tube.
*Evidence:* pose3, the region below the barrel from sheet x = 2470 rightward, y 300-350, is
clean background (`analysis/work_fa/p3_guard.png`); the tube's lower edge at x 2490 is y 289.
*Confidence:* 0.7

**FA-18 `frame.surface_features.count`** — `count` — **0** — DECLARED
The receiver body carries no modelled protrusion, recess, rivet, pin or panel line larger
than 0.02 L, other than the port (FA-3..7) and the rear spur (FA-9..11).
*Check:* count meshes parented under the receiver whose AABB diagonal exceeds 0.02 L,
excluding the port and spur. Must be 0.
*Evidence:* **DECLARED** — a simplicity choice, not a measurement. What I measured is that
nothing on the receiver exceeds 3 px in any pose, and that the orange marks at sheet
(2430-2440, 262-270) and (2425-2437, 282-292) classify in the same hue band as the tube's
rust graffiti, i.e. they are paint. The reference cannot distinguish "smooth" from
